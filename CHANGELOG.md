# Changelog

All notable changes to VLTN Tone Studio. Versions match `app/package.json` and the GitHub Releases. The release workflow copies the section of the version it publishes into the release notes.

## [Unreleased]

### Added
- **Solo scales.** See where to solo on the neck for the song's key. It opens on the minor pentatonic (the relative minor in a major key), with other scales, a root, box positions, and fret numbers or note names. It follows the pitch shift and the capo, shows in **This song** and from a **Solo scale** button in Lyrics & chords, and is saved with the song ([Solo scales](PERFORMANCE.md#solo-scales)).
- **Song maps.** A song map sets a song's sections, the drums and bass for each section, bass note fixes, timed chords and lyrics in one go. Export a song brief from **This song → Song map**, have it filled in, then import the reply; the import shows what will change and can be undone ([Song maps](SONG-MAP.md)).
- **Audio saves itself.** Generated drums and bass, imported tracks, rendered parts and stem splits in a song from the library are saved as soon as they exist, one save at a time and never while music plays. The Song header shows Saved, Saving… with the step, Not saved or Couldn't save, with a button to save or try again. Opening another song, or closing the window, asks first when audio isn't saved; a playlist that is playing never stops for it ([Song library](SONG-LIBRARY.md)).
- **Count in on sticks or a hi-hat.** The metronome and the count-in can now play a drum sound instead of the click: sticks, a closed hi-hat, kick and hat, or ride. Next to **Metronome** there are **Sound**, **Count-in** and a count-in sound menu, so the count-in is no longer hidden in **Metronome settings**. The count-in sound follows the metronome unless you pick one. Each song keeps its own choice, with a default under **Defaults for songs without their own**. The samples load when you choose the sound, never at Play, and the click is used for any beat whose sample is not ready ([Metronome](PERFORMANCE.md#prepare-a-song)).
- **More count-in sounds, and your own.** Next to the click there are now a cowbell and a woodblock, and a drummer set: sticks, pedal hat, hi-hat, open hat, ride and snare, plus patterns that change the drum on the beat: kick and hat, rock (kick on 1 and 3, snare on 2 and 4), toms from high to low, and a crash on one. The menus are grouped, and **Listen** plays one bar of the chosen sound at the song tempo without starting the song. **My samples** takes your own short hit (WAV, FLAC or MP3, at most 2 seconds) for the downbeat and for the other beats, bounced from a drum plugin; a slot left empty uses the click ([Click sounds](PERFORMANCE.md#prepare-a-song)).
- **Send the click as MIDI.** Under **Metronome settings**, **Send the click as MIDI** plays each metronome and count-in beat as a General MIDI drum note on channel 10 (or another channel) on an output you pick, such as a virtual port feeding a drum plugin in another app. An **Offset (ms)** of −100 to +100 makes up for that app's delay, and **Also play the click here** keeps or mutes the built-in sound. It has its own output, does not need Send cues, and never goes to the cue output unless you pick it. It is not tried on hardware yet ([Click sounds](PERFORMANCE.md#prepare-a-song)).
- **A tone chain for the generated drums.** The Band settings have a **Drum tone** next to the Bass tone. Add an outboard capture (a compressor, preamp, console or tape), a preamp capture and a space IR from TONE3000, or open files from this computer, or start from **Glued kit**, **Room kit** or **Recorded kit**. It renders when you press Generate (and in the preview), never during playback. A capture plays on the middle of the kit and the sides are kept, and a stereo room IR plays on each side. If a file is missing, the drums play clean and the panel says which one ([Generated drums and bass](PERFORMANCE.md#generated-drums-and-bass)).
- **Remove and edit MIDI cues in the MIDI tab.** Each row of Song MIDI cues has an Edit button and an X. The X takes the cue out of the chart, and Undo puts it back. Edit loads the command and time so you can change them and press Update cue. Both are off while cues are armed and the song plays. The misleading "chart editor below" text is gone. A pedal command sent as several messages, such as a bank select and a program change, has an X too, so a cue added twice can be removed; it is still edited in the chart text.
- **MIDI cues on song sections.** Set **When** to **On section** in Song MIDI cues and pick "Every Chorus" or "Chorus 2 only": the cue is sent at the start of each matching section, so a pedal can switch patch on every chorus. In the chart it is `{midi@section Chorus: pc 1 5}`. The cues follow the sections when you move or rename them, jumping to or looping a section sends them, and starting in the middle of a chorus brings in the chorus patch. The table shows "Every Chorus (3×)" with the times as a tooltip, and preflight warns about a section name that matches nothing ([MIDI pedals](MIDI-PEDALS.md#triggers-macros-and-song-midi-files)).
- **Practice mute.** Mute your own instrument once and it stays muted in every song, by hand, in a playlist and across gapless joins. Choose **Practice mute** on the Play tab, or bind **Practice mute: guitar** (and the other kinds) to a footswitch, key or page-turner. Muted rows show a Practice badge, a banner in Performance and stage mode has an Unmute button, and each song keeps its own saved mix ([Practice mute](PERFORMANCE.md#practice-mute)).
- **Bluetooth MIDI for the GP-150 and GP-180.** The Device screen has a *Connect Bluetooth MIDI* button that opens a BLE-MIDI link to the pedal (desktop chooser, pairing prompt, automatic reconnect) and the diagnostic report now includes the Bluetooth state and counters. Editing the pedal stays on USB, because a GP-180 on firmware 1.1.1 did not answer the editor handshake over Bluetooth. See `BLUETOOTH.md`.
- **Tune a song down or up.** A *Key* control in the Song screen's mixer moves every stem by up to 12 semitones either way without changing the tempo, so you can play along to a song in E♭ or D with your guitar in standard tuning. It uses the Rubber Band Library's finer (R3) engine in offline mode, which is the best quality available in the browser. The shift is computed in the background (a few seconds per stem and minute of music, with a percentage shown), playback carries on from the same second, *Reset* brings the original back without computing anything, and *Export stems* writes the tuned stems with the shift in the file name.
- **Tune the Tone Match proposal on the pedal.** A new *Tune it on the pedal* card under the proposal checks it against what the pedal really plays. You play up to six takes of about 15 s in the song's style; each one is recorded through the pedal's USB audio and compared with the song's guitar (the same texture distance the offline refinement uses, so the takes don't have to be the same notes). After each take the five Guitar EQ bands are corrected from the spectral gap and the amp's Gain from the gain score. It stops when the takes stop getting closer, keeps the best one, and *Keep these settings* puts them in the proposal. Everything plays through the Rig's live edits and nothing is written to a slot.
- **Fix a wrong bass note yourself.** Click a beat or a chord in the Band panel's beat strip, pick the note and an octave, and choose where it applies: one beat, one bar, up to the next chord change, or one chord everywhere in its section. Your notes win over the original bass and the chords, and the list under the strip removes them one at a time or all at once ([Generated drums and bass](PERFORMANCE.md#generated-drums-and-bass)).
- **Bar lines, sections and the original band.** Bar lines follow the chord changes and can shift partway through a song, for a short or long bar. Sections find verses and choruses that share chords and vocals, run on to the end so outros are found, name a bridge where the bass drops out, and call a loud section that does not repeat a chorus. The generated bass rests and the drums stop where the original does, and louder or quieter sections play one energy level up or down, with your own section settings winning. Run **Analyse again** on songs analysed before to get the new sections ([Prepare a song](PERFORMANCE.md#prepare-a-song)).
- **Generated bass follows the original bass.** When the song has a bass stem, the generated bass plays the notes the original bassist played on each beat, and the chords give the passing notes. A new **Bass notes from** setting in the Band settings switches to the chords' own bass, and the beat strip shows the bass note under each chord change where it differs from the chord. Songs analysed before this release are analysed again the next time they open. See [Generated drums and bass](PERFORMANCE.md#generated-drums-and-bass).
- **Each section gets its own bass part.** The Band panel's sections table has Bass part, Busy and Loud columns, and a More menu for octave, fills, hit the one, slides, dead notes and pickups. Auto picks a part from the section's name. Generated bass is set to the song's loudness, and the panel says how far under the song it sits. See [The bass part](PERFORMANCE.md#the-bass-part).
- **Use your own render of the drums or bass.** Export the generated parts as MIDI, render them in your own DAW, and use the audio in place of the generated track. The file is length-checked, can be lined up with the first generated note, and works with What plays, the mixer and backups. Back to generated renders the part again.
- **A bass tone chain for the generated bass.** The generated bass can play through up to six blocks in your order (pedal, preamp, amp, cab IR, space IR), each from TONE3000 or a file on this computer, with drive, output, mix and on/off per block, a dry/wet for the chain, presets to start from, and Fast render. The chain renders when you press Generate, not during playback, is matched to the clean bass's loudness, and a missing file leaves the clean bass with a notice. See [Generated drums and bass](PERFORMANCE.md#generated-drums-and-bass).
- **Better key estimates.** The key is read from the harmony, with the bass and the chords, which now run before it. Two sets of key weights are averaged, and a relative major or minor pair is decided by the first and last chords and the bass. The readout names a Mixolydian or Dorian flavour when the chords show one, and lists a section in another key, such as a last chorus a tone up. See [Performance](PERFORMANCE.md#lyrics-and-chords).
- **Better chord estimates.** Chords name power chords, sevenths, sus2 and sus4, diminished and augmented chords, with slash basses such as C/G. They are read from the guitar, piano and other stems together, and the bass stem gives the bass note. Chords change only on the beats and lean towards the song's key, so they flicker less. Songs analysed before this release are analysed again the next time they open or in a batch run. See [Performance](PERFORMANCE.md#lyrics-and-chords).
- **Every new song is analysed in one run.** After a split or an import, the analysis works out the tempo and beat, key, chords, sections, loudness, the drums and bass check, and lyrics, without opening the Performance screen. The Stems tab has an **After the split** block to choose the steps and the lyrics model before you split. The run goes on while you use other screens, and the status bar shows its progress. **Analyse this song now** runs the chosen steps again for a song that is already split. See [Saved songs](SONG-LIBRARY.md#analysing-saved-songs).
- **Lyrics from vocals is its own section** on the Performance screen, with the model and quality choice above the button. It used to be folded away under the chart editor.
- **Analysis in the background.** A saved-song analysis keeps going when you close its dialog or leave the Song screen. The status bar shows the song, the step, a bar and the time left on every screen, and the Library shows the same line. **Run in background** closes the dialog; reopening it shows the run, and the summary stays until **Done**. A run that waits for a playlist can be released with **Run anyway** when no song plays. See [Saved songs](SONG-LIBRARY.md#analysing-saved-songs).
- **Find the beat again from the tempo you set.** When a song's tempo was set by hand, tapped or taken from the chart, **Find the beat at N BPM** on the Performance screen finds the beat from that number, and **Keep exactly N** keeps the tempo as typed. **Undo** puts back the tempo, the grid and the sections. The Band panel's **Find the beat** starts from it too, with **Use my tempo**. See [Performance](PERFORMANCE.md).
- **Playlist editing with Save.** A playlist's edits are a draft until **Save playlist** (or Cmd/Ctrl+S), with **Discard changes** and a prompt before leaving unsaved edits. **Add songs…** adds songs inside a playlist, or to the end of a set. Pressing Enter creates a playlist, and **New playlist from selected…** makes one from the songs ticked in the Library. Playlist changes are never dropped while another one is saving. See [Saved songs](SONG-LIBRARY.md#saved-songs-and-live-playlists).
- **Suggested sections.** The analysis suggests a song's sections (intro, verse, chorus, bridge, solo and outro) on the bar lines, from its chords, loudness and vocals. A song with no sections takes them; a song with its own keeps them, and the section editor offers to replace them, with **Undo**. Songs analysed earlier are analysed once more to fill in their sections. The suggestions are a starting point to check by ear. See [Saved songs](SONG-LIBRARY.md#analysing-saved-songs).
- **Analyse saved songs again.** Songs saved before the analysis have a wrong tempo, and a detected tempo can be half or double the real one. On the Performance screen, **Detect** beside the BPM finds the tempo again and offers the half and the double, with **Undo**; **Analyse again** on the analysis line runs every step again. In the Library, **Analyse selected…** and **Analyse all songs…** analyse saved songs without opening them. They skip the open song, wait while a song plays, and report the tempos they changed. See [Saved songs](SONG-LIBRARY.md#analysing-saved-songs).
- **Compare backups with the pedal, and keep only recent backups.** A restore compares each slot with the pedal and writes only the changed slots by default (*Write all* is still there). Changed slots show the block-level difference. A backup's menu in the Library has *Compare with pedal* (read-only) and *Pin backup*. *Settings › Keep backups* can keep every backup, the newest 10 or 30 per pedal, or the last 90 days; older backups move to the Trash after each backup, and pinned ones are never moved. Desktop app only for pruning.
- **Generated drums and bass.** The Band panel finds the beat and writes drum and bass parts that follow its chords and sections, in styles with complexity, swing, humanise, and a follow-my-playing option. The parts are normal tracks in the mixer, and the notes can be exported as a MIDI file. It works offline, and never during playback. See [Performance](PERFORMANCE.md#generated-drums-and-bass).
- **Choose original or generated drums and bass.** For each part, *What plays* picks the original, the generated part or both, for a song that has the part already as well as one that does not. The switch works during playback, and the choice is saved with the song and carried to similar songs. Remote and MIDI bindings can toggle each part. See [Performance](PERFORMANCE.md#generated-drums-and-bass).
- **Band sync.** Bandmates on phones or tablets on the same local network follow the song, lyrics and chords, with their own text size and transpose. Off until you start it, PIN-protected and LAN-only, with optional remote play, pause and stop. The traffic is not encrypted. See [Performance](PERFORMANCE.md#band-sync).
- **Audience display.** A second window shows the lyrics and chords, or a PDF page, on a projector or second screen, following playback. Choose the display (the app remembers it, and uses the main screen while it is unplugged), with chords, text size, rotation and blackout. Closing it never stops playback. The browser build opens the same view in a pop-up. See [Performance](PERFORMANCE.md#audience-display).
- **PDF charts.** A saved song can show a PDF (lead sheet, chord chart or score) instead of its text chart. Scroll or one-page layouts (two pages side by side on a wide screen), pen, highlighter and text annotations saved with the song, page turns at song times that follow playback, and remote page actions that turn the PDF in stage mode too. The PDF and its annotations go into backups. See [Performance](PERFORMANCE.md#pdf-charts).
- **Import ChordPro and chords above lyrics.** The chart editor reads ChordPro (`.cho`, `.chordpro`, `.pro`, `.crd`) and plain chords-above-lyrics text, with a summary of what was read and of each line that was kept as a comment. Headings, `//` comments and `{color:…}` spans are supported in the chart. See [Lyrics and chords](PERFORMANCE.md#lyrics-and-chords).
- **Chart editor search, undo and colours.** The chart text box has search and replace (Cmd/Ctrl+F, Cmd/Ctrl+H), undo and redo, and colours for timestamps, chords, headings, comments, colour tags, MIDI commands, macros and directives. Remote keys do not run while it has focus. See [Performance](PERFORMANCE.md#lyrics-and-chords).
- **Autoscroll styles.** Follow playback can move the lyrics line by line, smoothly between timed lines, or at a constant speed in lines per minute with a fit to the song's length. A manual or remote scroll pauses it for six seconds. See [Lyrics and chords](PERFORMANCE.md#lyrics-and-chords).
- **Perform a set.** Song → Performance plays stems with a song-following metronome, a combined lyrics and chords reader with guitar chord tabs, and an estimated song key. See [Performance](PERFORMANCE.md).
- **Timed MIDI cues.** Program changes, controllers, notes and SysEx fire at set times in a song, over USB or standard Bluetooth MIDI, with a pedal command library (TimeLine, RC-10R, IR-200, SY-200, GP-150, GP-180), custom pedal import and export, a one-time GP-180 tempo command and a preflight check. See [MIDI pedal command presets](MIDI-PEDALS.md).
- **Song library and playlists.** Songs are saved with their audio, stems, chart, lyrics and mix, and ordered into playlists that play in sequence. A whole-playlist check and portable backup and restore are included. See [Song library](SONG-LIBRARY.md).
- **Count-in, pre-roll and tap tempo.** A one- or two-bar count-in, a pre-roll, an optional start delay, four metronome modes, a beat light and tap tempo for the song BPM. See [Performance](PERFORMANCE.md).
- **Song sections.** Name the parts of a song (Intro, Verse, Chorus …) and control them live: tap a section to jump to it at the next boundary, double-tap to jump now, loop a section until released, and set what happens at each section's end (continue, repeat once, loop until released, skip or pause). Sections are saved with the song. In Stage they replace the seek bar. See [Performance](PERFORMANCE.md#sections).
- **Stage mode.** Large lyrics and transport, locked editing and seeking, always-visible MIDI status with a Panic button, and sleep prevention during playback.
- **GP-150 and GP-180 performance MIDI** with setup presets that cover both models.
- **Audio outputs.** Route the main mix, a click bus and two aux buses to chosen outputs of your interface, with a separate click device, a test tone per bus and a bus for each track. See [Audio outputs](PERFORMANCE.md#audio-outputs).
- **Gapless playlists and crossfade.** The next song loads in the background, so most joins are gapless. A crossfade of up to 10 seconds and a **Crossfade to next** button are included. Each song has trim (with Auto), fades and an end behaviour: play the next song, stop the playlist or loop. See [Gapless and crossfade](PERFORMANCE.md#gapless-and-crossfade).
- **Speed and pitch.** Play at 50–150% speed, shift pitch by up to ±6 semitones with fine tune, and set a capo. Each track can follow the pitch change or keep its own, and the metronome, count-in and tempo commands use the effective BPM. See [Speed and pitch](PERFORMANCE.md#speed-and-pitch).
- **Multitrack import.** Add up to 32 audio files or a folder as extra tracks, with mute groups and per-track settings. Songs can be normalised to −14 LUFS, and track settings can be applied to similar songs. See [Multitrack import](PERFORMANCE.md#multitrack-import).
- **Remote control.** Live actions (transport, songs, sections, lyrics scroll and text size, stage mode, mute groups, click, tap tempo, speed and arming) can run from a MIDI message, a key or a Bluetooth page-turner. Learn binds the next pedal message or key, shows its channel and names any clash, and a double-press option guards Stop and Panic. Start, Continue, Stop and CC 80 are now default bindings. See [Remote control](PERFORMANCE.md#remote-control).
- **Load songs from MIDI.** A program change (with its bank) or Song Select on the selected input loads the saved song it names. Each song's trigger is learned or typed in the performance view, and the options choose whether songs can load from the whole library or the current playlist, what a trigger does while a song plays (ignore, arm as next, or stop and load now), and whether the song starts. Clashes between songs or with a remote action are named in the view and in playlist preflight. See [Load songs from MIDI](PERFORMANCE.md#load-songs-from-midi).
- **Pedal state on mid-song start.** Starting or seeking into a song, or recovering from a stall, now sets each pedal to the patch and controller values in force at that point. Trigger controls such as looper and tap tempo are left out, and `{chase}` / `{nochase}` on a cue override the defaults.
- **Compact song library.** Saved songs are stored as lossless FLAC instead of raw float32, about half the disk space, and load straight from disk without passing the audio through the app's messaging layer. Songs saved by earlier versions are converted in the background at the next start, and old backups still restore. After conversion an older version of the app can no longer open the library. See [Song library](SONG-LIBRARY.md).
- **Library organisation, queue and history.** Songs gain a key, year, keywords, notes, colour and folder, and the library can be searched, sorted, filtered and batch-edited. Playlists can be split into named sets, and smart playlists pick their songs by rules. The queue shows and edits what plays next without disturbing the song playing, each entry can end, loop or wait for Play, set times use the trimmed length, and a play is recorded at its halfway point in a history that can make a playlist. The library saves as version 4, which older versions refuse to open. See [Song library](SONG-LIBRARY.md).
- **Bluetooth link resilience.** Bluetooth MIDI now packs messages that fall within a few milliseconds into one packet (stamped with their intended send time), so a busy stream no longer needs a write per message. If the pedal goes out of range or drops the link, the app reconnects on its own with a growing delay, without the device chooser; the song stays disarmed until you rearm it. **Stop reconnecting** is next to the status.
- **Library hygiene.** Restoring a backup can skip songs that are already in the library, so restoring twice no longer duplicates songs or playlists. A saved song's chart, BPM, lyrics and options now live only in the library, no longer also in local storage, and earlier copies are moved over. Leftover folders from interrupted saves are cleaned up automatically. See [Song library](SONG-LIBRARY.md).
- **MIDI timecode.** Sends MIDI Time Code at 24, 25 or 30 fps (non-drop): a full frame at start, seek, jump, loop and speed change, then quarter frames that follow song time. Each song has an `HH:MM:SS:FF` offset, validated against the rate. Timecode replaces app MIDI clock, is not recommended over Bluetooth (preflight warns), and is silent during the count-in. See [Performance](PERFORMANCE.md#midi-timecode).
- **MIDI over the network.** The guide now explains macOS Network MIDI (no code needed) and Windows RTP-MIDI setup, so network sessions are usable as ordinary ports. See [MIDI pedal command presets](MIDI-PEDALS.md#midi-over-the-network).
- **Play history shows sets.** A play made in a playlist set shows its playlist and set name (for example "Gig › Set 1") next to the song. See [Song library](SONG-LIBRARY.md).
- **MIDI triggers, macros and song MIDI files.** Charts can send commands on load, play, stop or end. Saved command lists (macros) are shared by every song, and a song can attach a standard MIDI file that plays with its cues at the song's speed. Cues also accept bank select, song select and Start/Continue/Stop. Preflight flags Start and Stop cues while app MIDI clock is on. See [MIDI pedal command presets](MIDI-PEDALS.md#triggers-macros-and-song-midi-files).

### Changed
- **Songs skip the silence at their start by default.** A song with no trim start of its own now starts on its first sound, after the silence the song analysis found. Set **Trim start** to 0 to play from the very start of the file.
- **The Band dialog keeps its buttons in view.** Generate, Try another, Preview, Remove and Export MIDI sit in a bar at the bottom, with the progress, Cancel and notices beside them. After Generate the bar says it is done and offers Close and Play from the selected section. Preview now takes any section, or the playhead, for the whole section, 4 or 8 bars ([Generated drums and bass](PERFORMANCE.md#generated-drums-and-bass)).
- **Send cues stays on.** Switching on **Send cues** is remembered for the output you chose, so it stays on for every song, tab switch and restart, and arms again when that output reconnects (Bluetooth or USB). The MIDI summary says "(remembered)" when the app armed itself. Choosing another output disarms, and you switch it on again for the new one. A fault still disarms at once and shows why, but keeps Send cues on for the next song or reconnect. **Panic / disarm** turns it off ([Performance](PERFORMANCE.md)).
- **Lyrics start from the analysis, not from opening the lyrics panel.** The lyrics panel no longer starts transcription when it opens, and its "Transcribe lyrics when analysing" box is replaced by the lyrics step under **After the split** on the Stems tab. The panel's own buttons are as they were.
- GP-150 and GP-180 USB editor sessions are no longer read-only. Bluetooth is for performance MIDI only; Bluetooth editor management is disabled because the GP-180 gave no replies to its handshake.

### Changed
- **Pick the Valeton Suite again after updating.** Only a path chosen in the file picker is trusted now, so the path stored by an earlier version no longer counts: Settings shows no Suite until you choose it with *Choose…*, and *Open Valeton Suite* does nothing until then (on macOS the default install in Applications still works with no path). The app no longer opens or runs any other path the page asks for: only folders it hands out itself (logs, the library) and files you picked in a dialog.
- **Deleting in the Library moves things to the Trash.** Deleted collections, presets and capture versions go to the system Trash (a `.trash` folder in the library if the Trash refuses, emptied of anything older than 30 days), so a mis-click can be undone. The dialogs and toasts say so. The web build still deletes for good and says that.
- **Window and file safety.** The main window runs sandboxed, and MIDI, SysEx and audio capture are granted only to Tone Studio's own pages.

### Fixed
- **Generated bass plays in an outro after the drums stop.** The beat grid ends where the drums stop, so a section after that got no generated parts. The band now carries the beat on through any section that starts after the grid's last beat, so an outro with no drums still gets its bass.
- **A bass note you set by hand always plays.** A lead-in into the next section, a fill or a pedal no longer plays over it, and a note whose time is a few milliseconds off its beat (as song maps round them) still lands on that beat.
- **A part split no longer cuts a song short.** Splitting only part of a song for Tone Match left Performance and the Band panel with stems just that long. Performance now plays the whole song without them and says why, and the part's stems are never saved.
- **Bluetooth keeps messages sent together in order.** Two messages sent at the same moment over Bluetooth could reach the pedal second one first, because one took a shortcut past the other's timer. On a GP-180 that showed as a program change landing 65 to 158 ms apart from its partner. Timed messages now wait in one queue, in timestamp order.
- **Songs from an old backup open straight after a restore.** Restoring a backup made by an early version used to leave its songs unopenable until the app was restarted. They are now converted right away, with progress, as the restore finishes.
- **Saving a song no longer loses tracks.** A track that could not be read when the song opened is kept as saved instead of being deleted on the next save, and an unreadable source is never replaced by a mix of the stems.
- **A song put back from the Trash comes back.** The library used to delete a restored song folder again within ten minutes. It now adds the song back to the library.
- **A power cut during a save no longer loses the song.** The saved files and the library list are written to disk before the old copy is removed.
- **Settings and library files survive a crash or a full disk.** Settings, collection details, tone records, capture drafts and the window position are written to a temp file and renamed into place, one save at a time, so a crash can no longer leave half a file or lose a change. A settings file that can't be read is kept as `settings.corrupt-<date>.json` instead of being overwritten with defaults, and only known settings with the right type are kept. On Windows, a rename that fails because Defender or an indexer holds the file is retried for about a second.
- **A pedal backup is never listed half done.** A backup is built in a temp folder and moved into place only when every file is in, and Restore refuses one that is missing files. If the final move fails, the finished copy is kept and the error says where it is. Left-over temp folders are removed an hour later.
- **Writes can't drop unsaved edits, and stale backup copies are flagged.** Writing a preset to another slot while the current one has unsaved edits is refused. The write dialog warns when the backup copy it would use may be older than what is on the pedal. Progress messages use the pedal's own slot names (GP-200 family *12-C*).
- **Downloads can no longer hang.** A stalled separation model or TONE3000 download fails after about a minute and a model download picks up where it stopped. The Song screen has a *Cancel* next to the model progress. Download errors keep their own message (not found, signed out, rate limited) instead of the generic one.
- **TONE3000 sign-in is kept private.** The token goes only to TONE3000 over https, including across redirects. On Linux without a keyring the token stays in memory for the run instead of being kept on disk with a fixed key, and Settings says so. TONE3000 images are limited to 10 MB and an image type, and the image cache is capped at 50 MB.
- **Closing the window on macOS doesn't break the app.** Reopening it works, and a preset opened from Finder while no window is open is queued instead of lost. Clicking *Download* twice no longer starts two update downloads.
- **GP-150 / GP-180 record levels accept the pedal's −21 dB.** A GP-180 reported its Bluetooth and Mic record levels as −21 dB, which the app declared out of range, so the value was shown wrong and could not be set back. Their range is now −21…+20 dB.
- **GP-180 editor writes are checked on a real pedal.** Renaming a preset, saving it to a slot, reading it back and changing a pedal setting over USB all worked on a GP-180 (firmware 1.1.1). Uploading a User IR was not tried.
- **Mac downloads open again.** Builds without a signing certificate now carry a valid ad-hoc signature, so macOS no longer calls them damaged. The first time, macOS still asks you to confirm in System Settings › Privacy & Security › Open Anyway, until the app is notarized.
- **Lyrics transcription works again.** Since 0.9.2 every transcription, for one song or from the Library, failed with "window is not defined", because the lyrics worker loaded the song settings. A test now loads every worker without a page, so this can't come back unnoticed.
- **Chords, key and sections work in the installed app.** Three analysis workers were blocked in packaged builds, so every song said "Could not work out: chords, key, sections". A test now guards how the workers are created.
- **Lyrics catch the opening verse.** The vocal detector measured against the loudest moment, so a quieter start was skipped. Lines no longer share a time or break mid-phrase, and music markers (♪) are dropped; pasted lyrics are timed from their best-matched words ([Lyrics and chords](PERFORMANCE.md#lyrics-and-chords)).
- **Lyrics still work when graphics acceleration hears nothing.** The app retries on the CPU, remembers that for this machine and version, and goes straight to the CPU next time. An empty result says which runtime heard what.
- **The Band panel and mixer are easier to use.** The panel fits the window on long songs and keeps chord names after Generate, and generated parts no longer reset a song's saved analysis. What plays now shows the part that actually plays, and the mixer marks the tracks it silences, uses the Stems tab's names and lines its rows up ([Generated drums and bass](PERFORMANCE.md#generated-drums-and-bass)).
- **The song screen is easier to read.** The header shows the saved song's title and artist, and the key readout and queue count are clearer.
- **Normalising keeps the song's peak below full scale.** A song measured at −19.8 LUFS with a −3 dBFS peak was raised by +5.8 dB and clipped at the device. The gain is now also limited so the song's peak stays at or below −1 dBFS, and the control says when the peak set it. Songs normalised by earlier versions are measured again the next time you turn normalising on.
- **A playlist join uses the next song's count-in.** A song with a one-bar count-in after a song without one joined gaplessly, with no count-in. The count-in clicks now start at the outgoing song's end, at the next song's own tempo and count-in length.
- **Imported tracks without a split no longer blank the Stems tab.** A library song with imported tracks but no split stems opened the Stems tab onto a blank window. The tab now shows the split prompt, so the song can be split.
- **Scrolling over a knob scrolls the page.** A knob on the rig turned with the mouse wheel or trackpad wherever the pointer was, so scrolling the page could change a setting, such as a global like the Mic record level, and send it to the pedal. A knob now turns with the wheel only after you click or Tab to it. Dragging and keys work as before.
- **A set keeps playing on another Song tab.** Switching from Performance to Songs & playlists, Stems or Tone match hid the Performance panel and stopped the set, so its queue could not be edited while it played. The panel now stays mounted and is hidden: the set plays on, the queue can be edited, and the Performance tab shows the same position when you return. While Performance is hidden, only transport and song keys run; other keys, such as Page Down, the arrows, stage mode and tap tempo, are left to the page. Leaving the Song screen still pauses the set.
- Pausing a set and pressing Play resumes the set. A set of one song showed "Play song" and resumed as a single song, so the play was recorded without its set. The button reads "Play playlist" while a set is loaded and paused, and Pause, a seek, Next and Previous keep the set, so a play counted afterwards records it.
- The Performance status reads "Playlist waiting for Play" when the next song's entry asks to wait for Play, instead of "Playlist playing", until Play starts it.
- Autoscroll follows playback once the text chart replaces a PDF chart. The lyrics stayed at the top until the text size changed, because the row positions were measured while the PDF was shown. They are now measured again when the text chart appears and when the lyrics change size, such as after a font loads or the window resizes.
- Bluetooth MIDI keeps messages sent with the same timestamp in order. A message sent a moment after an earlier one, with a timestamp the earlier one was still waiting for, could be written first. Messages now wait in timestamp order, so a pedal's program-change pair arrives in the order the song sent it.
- MIDI timecode now runs to a playlist join. The outgoing song sent its quarter frames only until the hand-over, about half a second early, so a receiver freewheeled across the join. Its quarter frames now run to the join, and the next song's full frame follows them. A pause, stop or seek straight after the join stops them.
- **PDF page turns move in scroll layout.** A remote or key page turn (Page Down, a learned pedal, Next page) now scrolls a PDF in scroll layout at once, in stage mode and outside it, with no animation to stall in a window that is not painting. Half a page in stage, nine tenths of a page otherwise.
- Soloing a muted track in a mute group now plays it alone. The solo also unmutes that track and mutes the rest of its group, the same as an unmute does. Before, the track's solo was set but it stayed silent, because the mute won.
- Importing tracks into a song that has no split keeps the song's own audio as a track, so it still plays and stays in the mixer beside the imported tracks. Before, the mixer and playback dropped it once tracks were added.
- Quitting the app no longer shows an "Object has been destroyed" error. The audience display closes after the performance window has gone, and its status and the other messages sent to that window during quit are now skipped instead of throwing.
- A song's `@stop` and `@end` triggers now reach the pedal over Bluetooth MIDI. They were dropped when the output was cleared straight after a pause, a playlist stop or the end of a song, so only USB sent them.
- A section loop or jump no longer sends the cue at its boundary twice. The cue at the old end of the passage is not sent, and a chase leaves out the patch and controllers that the cue at the new position sets, so the pedal is left on the right patch.
- A speed change no longer re-sends the pedals' patch and controller state. The song position does not move at a speed swap, so the pedals already have that state.
- With app MIDI clock on and no count-in, Start and the first clock pulses go out on time at the start of the audio. The audio is held back 150 ms, so the pulses are not sent as one burst. A start with a count-in is unchanged.
- **A set warns at once when its next song cannot be loaded.** The next song loads in the background while the current one plays. If that load fails, the Performance view shows the song's name and the reason straight away, instead of only when the set reaches the join. The set still stops at the join, and the warning clears when the next song changes or loads.
- The song BPM field takes a typed value. Typing keeps the text, and it is committed (clamped to 30–300) on blur or Enter; Escape restores the current BPM. Stepping with the arrows still sets the value at once.
- A Bluetooth MIDI command that arrives more than 250 ms late is now dropped and counted instead of closing the Bluetooth connection.
- Pause and panic only release channels that sent notes, so they no longer trigger the GP-180 looper Stop.
- A stall no longer disarms MIDI for the rest of the song. Late program changes, controllers and notes are dropped (note-offs are always sent), the schedule re-syncs, and a notice shows what was dropped. USB cues are queued up to a second ahead with exact timestamps. MIDI only disarms when timing cannot recover.
- GP-180 backups keep the connected pedal model rather than defaulting to GP-150.
- Our own scheduled MIDI that comes back through a THRU input is now recognised by its wire time. A USB cue is queued up to a second early, so its echo used to be missed and could fire a binding. The echo window is 200 ms.
- On the GP-150 and GP-180, cues for other pedals now go out before the pedal's own, so they reach the other pedals on time instead of after the pedal's next message.
- Saved songs no longer fail to load with "Missing or damaged audio".
- Starting or seeking mid-song no longer replays controller messages that a pedal profile assigns to triggers (such as the RC-10R looper). Preflight warns about a controller it cannot tell apart from a trigger, and says how to decide.
- Starting mid-song waits 100 ms after the pedal state is sent before the audio starts, and Start and Continue are sent after it.
- Playlist joins no longer send MIDI Stop, and the set keeps playing with its USB output armed across a join instead of pausing.
- The next playlist song is preloaded even when it has no stem split, so joins into plain backing tracks are gapless.
- Chord names follow the song's key override (or the library key) when transposed or using a capo, so a flat key shows Eb rather than D#.
- USB MIDI is queued at most 0.2 s ahead, so Pause stops queued cues almost at once.
- MIDI tokens inside `//` comment lines in a chart are ignored, so they no longer break the chart.
- Each pause or stop sends one MIDI Stop, and each resume sends the play trigger.
- The USB MIDI output recovers after the computer sleeps. A dead port disarms with a message instead of silently sending nothing.
- A slow-starting audio output no longer disarms cues as a stall.
- A steady audio offset of 30 ms or more is followed after three checkpoints, so cues and MIDI clock stay on the audio instead of running early for the whole song. It no longer uses up the resync margin for later hitches.
- A speed change or section jump that falls due as the audio passes it is always carried out, so the MIDI clock and cues move to the new rate and position instead of staying on the old one.
- A song loaded by hand, or by a program change or remote binding, keeps the USB or system MIDI access, output and input of the song before it. Song triggers and remote bindings keep working after a load. Cues start disarmed, as before, until you arm them.
- Play no longer disarms the cues after the computer sleeps. The scheduler worker reads the same clock as the audio, and a worker from before the sleep is replaced on wake, so cues stay armed on the first Play after a long sleep.
- Key bindings work in stage mode, and while a checkbox or button has focus. Page Down, the arrows, T and a learned key ran nothing in stage mode, and a focused checkbox or button after a click swallowed them. Stage mode no longer blocks them, and a text field, a slider or another open dialog still does.
- **Lyrics from before 0.9.2 are no longer skipped silently.** The Library's Lyrics step treats those charts as unknown, not yours, and the dialog says how many songs it will skip and why, before the run starts. Tick **Include lyrics from before version 0.9.2** to transcribe them; each song keeps its previous lyrics for **Restore the previous lyrics**. The list and summary give each song's lyrics result, including failures with their message.

- **The GP-50's own effects.** On a GP-50 the model picker offers PRE AC Sim, PRE Cry Wah and the CAB AC (Dreadnought) cabinet, and delay and modulation pedals show their *Sync* switch. A GP-5 doesn't show them.
- **Presets across models.** A GP-5 preset written to a GP-50 is converted without loss. A GP-50 preset written or restored to a GP-5 loses its GP-50-only models, and the confirmation lists them first. Restore accepts backups of either model.
- **Valeton GP-200, GP-200LT and GP-200JR support.** Tone Studio connects to the GP-200 family (a GP-200R or GP-200X reports as a GP-200) and runs every screen on it:
  - *Rig*: a flat chain where every block moves, the FX loop's send and return too; CTRL 1–8 assignments per preset; patch settings (EXP 1A/1B/2, Quick Access 1–3, pan, FX loop, drum style, author, note); tempo 40–250 BPM with tap, sent to the pedal live.
  - *Library*: 256 slots (255 on the JR) labelled like the pedal, `01-A` to `64-D`, with 16-character names and *Reset to init patch*. `.prst` files are 1224 bytes; 1176-byte files from older editors open too.
  - *Tones*: 10 SnapTone slots (0–4 play in AMP, 5–9 in DST) and 20 User IR slots of 1024 samples, written over USB.
  - *Device*: the pedal settings of each model and the Global EQ. The MIDI monitor decodes the family's messages.
- **GP-200 effect list.** Valeton's effect names and knob ranges can't ship with Tone Studio. *Device › GP-200 effect list* reads `algorithm.xml` from your GP-200 editor (*Find automatically* in the desktop app, or *Choose file…*) and keeps it on your computer. Without it, effects show as numbers and their knobs can't be edited.
- **Experimental pedal commands.** *Settings › Experimental pedal commands* turns on commands known only from Valeton's software, not yet tried on a real pedal: on a GP-50, EXP settings, live tempo, footswitch and chain changes, reading a slot without selecting it, and deleting, copying and moving presets inside the pedal; on the GP-200 family, the Global EQ. It is off by default; the simulator always has them.
- **Simulated pedals.** *Device › Simulate* offers every supported model, and the browser build opens them with `?mock=gp50`, `?mock=gp200`, `?mock=gp200lt` and `?mock=gp200jr`.
- **A cab IR inside a SnapTone.** When a NAM capture becomes a SnapTone on a GP-5 or GP-50 (Tones or the capture editor), you can pick a cab IR: a downloaded TONE3000 IR or a WAV file. Tone Studio plays the capture through the IR before measuring it, at the dry capture's level, so amp-only captures sound complete where an engaged SnapTone bypasses CAB. Captures that already include the cab (TONE3000 *Amp + cab*, or the file's gear type *Amp + cab* / *Pedal + amp + cab*) offer no cab IR, and neither does the GP-200 family, where CAB keeps playing after the SnapTone.

### Changed
- Texts name the connected pedal (GP-5, GP-50 or a GP-200 model) instead of always saying GP-5. Backups go into `<model>-YYYY-MM-DD` folders (`gp50-…`, `gp200-…`) titled after the model; `backup.json` records it. Diagnostic reports are saved as `valeton-diagnostics-*.txt`.
- SnapTone and User IR links of a tone remember which model they were made on, and the Tones screen only treats a link as "on your pedal" when the model matches.
- The README is rewritten for every supported model: a section on the GP-200 family, a support table for the GP-5, GP-50, GP-200 family and GP-150 (not supported), install notes and the simulators.

### Known limitations
- The GP-50 path hasn't been tried on a real GP-50 by this project yet. Reading, writing and Program Change select on a GP-50 come from drewmerc302/valeton-gp50, which tested them on hardware. Live edits, SnapTone and User IR uploads use the protocol the two pedals share; Valeton Suite 2.1.0's own code sends a GP-50 the same bytes.
- No GP-200, GP-200LT or GP-200JR has been tried by this project yet. The GP-200 family's protocol comes from Valeton's GP-200 editor (static analysis of version 1.8.0); the LT and JR are known only from its code.
- The GP-200LT and GP-200JR are read-only on a real unit until one has been tested: Tone Studio reads them, backs them up and plays live edits, but doesn't write presets, restore, change settings or upload SnapTones and IRs. Whether they have SnapTone slots isn't known yet.
- The GP-200 Global EQ isn't read from the pedal: the panel shows what the pedal reports while connected, over the editor's factory values.
- Tone Match isn't available on the GP-200 family: it proposes presets from the GP-5 and GP-50 effects.

## [0.4.0] - 2026-10-10

### Added
- **In-app updates.** When Tone Studio starts and finds a newer release on GitHub, it asks whether to download and install it. On Windows and with the AppImage, *Download and install* fetches the update, checks it against the SHA-512 published with the release, and restarts into the new version. The progress shows in the dialog, and *Hide* lets you keep working while it downloads. On macOS and with the `.deb`, the dialog offers the release page instead.
- **Safe restarts.** The app never restarts for an update while it is writing to or backing up the GP-5. If the preset has unsaved changes, it asks first. *Later* or *Install when I quit* installs a downloaded update the next time you close the app.
- **Update controls.** *Help › Check for updates…*, the command palette and the About dialog check right away. *Settings › About* shows the version and the update status. It also has *Check for updates automatically* (on by default: at launch and every six hours). Nothing is downloaded until you agree.

### Changed
- Releases also publish `latest.yml`, `latest-linux.yml` and the installer blockmaps, which the app updates from. Each release is created as a draft and only published once every file is uploaded. The workflow stops if the update files are missing or name another version.

### Known limitations
- 0.4.0 is the first version that can update itself. To get it, download it from the Releases page; from 0.4.0 on, the app offers new versions by itself.
- macOS builds aren't signed yet, so macOS can't install updates in place. The app opens the release page instead.

## [0.3.0] - 2026-10-10

### Added
- **User IRs over USB.** Tone Studio writes cab IRs straight to the GP-5's 20 User IR slots, without Valeton Suite, on Windows, macOS and Linux. It works for IR tones from TONE3000, for a `.wav` you drop on the window or open, and for the IR made by Tone Match. The WAV is prepared the way Suite does it: first channel, resampled to 44.1 kHz, 24-bit, first 512 samples (11.6 ms, all the GP-5 keeps). Before you write to an occupied slot, the app shows what you'll replace.
- **TONE3000 app key in Settings.** Enter your publishable key under *Settings › TONE3000 account*. If you change it, the app signs you out. With no key set, Tones links to Settings.
- **Tone Match is easier to find.** It's in the command palette (*Tone match*, *Split a song into stems*), and after a split the stems view shows a *Match the guitar tone* button. The analysis starts as soon as the split finishes.

### Changed
- *Sign in to TONE3000* now signs you in and comes straight back; you no longer have to pick a tone first. *Browse TONE3000* still opens the tone picker.
- Tones opens on the *Trending* list.
- User IR slots are numbered 1–20 everywhere, like on the pedal and in the Rig.
- The Song screen remembers whether you were on Stems or Tone match.
- *Settings › Valeton Suite* is only used to find the SnapTone test signal and for firmware updates, and it's hidden on Linux.
- Builds made locally with `npm run dist` include Valeton's SnapTone test signal when it's in the git-ignored fixtures folder. Published builds still ask for the file once.

### Removed
- The Valeton Suite hand-off for IRs: the *Ready for Valeton Suite* folder, *Open Valeton Suite* and linking the slot after Suite.

### Known limitations
- Overwriting a User IR slot that already holds an IR hasn't been tested on a GP-5 yet. The pedal can't send an IR back, so keep the WAV of anything you replace.
